4. Next steps

The goal is to make yes easy: one short call, one checklist, and emails they can forward so nobody has to figure anything out.

  1. They respond

    Reply on LinkedIn, text Dave, or use the form on the page. The form opens an email to Dave with their name, company, role, store count and first store's city.

  2. 15-minute call, booked on the spot

    Dave confirms which store, who pays the utility bill and who services the refrigeration. Before hanging up, the next step and date are set.

  3. Data for one store

    The checklist below goes out the same day, with ready-to-forward emails for their facilities team and accounting.

  4. Store review

    Expected savings for that store, by piece of equipment, with every assumption written down.

  5. Treat one store and measure it

    Before and after, adjusted for weather, so their team can check the math.

  6. Roll out

    Plan the remaining stores, with financing options so savings can cover the payment, and add the stacked measures that pay for themselves.

Call agenda (15 minutes)

  • What's keeping them up at night: energy bills, breakdowns, replacing equipment, or all three
  • Their role, so we know who else needs to see it (owner, finance, facilities)
  • Which store to start with, and roughly how old its refrigeration is
  • Who has the bills and who services the equipment
  • Book the next touch before ending the call

Data checklist for one store

  • 12 months of electric bills (24 is better), every page
  • Nameplate photos: rack systems, condensing units, walk-in coolers and freezers, rooftop units
  • Rough age of the equipment, if known
  • Store hours and any big changes in the last two years
  • If they can download it, 15-minute interval data from the utility account

If photos don't come back within a week, offer to have a technician take them.
